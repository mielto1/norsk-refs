X Dictionary Entryにがて苦手にがてpoor (at); weak (in); not very good (at) [na adj., noun]Add to Your StudylistDelete Other Note にがて苦手にがてななほう方ほう You might have initially read the 方 here as かた. かた is an honorific equivalent of 人 (ひと), and it is indeed a common reading for this character. For example: つぎ次つぎののかた方かた、どうぞどうぞ。The next person, please. However, the reading かた does not fit for the sentence from the episode because, as an honorific, it's not appropriate to use to refer to yourself. You only call people outside your group かた. Instead, this is the word ほう, and in this sentence, it means "a side." The narrator is using a pattern in which the world is divided up into two sides, and then something is placed on one side or the other. You can use this pattern to give a rough approximation of where something is on any continuum: more this way, or more that way. For example, suppose you're considering laptops at a store. You comment that the price seems a little high. Your friend might say: このこのラップトップラップトップははまだまだやす安やすいいほうほうだだよよ。たか高たかいいののははこっちこっちだだ。This laptop is still on the cheaper side. The expensive ones are over here. In other words, if we line up all the laptops in the world and then divide them into "cheap" and "expensive" sides, this laptop falls on the cheap side. It doesn't mean that it's the absolute cheapest, just that, all things considered, it is relatively cheaper; on the cheaper side. Here's another example: わたし私わたしははクラスクラスののなか中なかでで、あし足あしががはや速はやいいほう方ほうだだととおも思おもうう。Within the class, I think my feet are on the faster side (=I can run relatively quickly). Again, the speaker is not saying they are the fastest in the class, or even close. It merely means that, dividing the class into faster runners and slower runners, the speaker is on the faster side. Similarly, in the sentence from the episode, the narrator is saying that, when you line up people based on how good they are at interacting with people, he is on the "not-so-good" side. Maybe not the worst, but definitely not the best.
